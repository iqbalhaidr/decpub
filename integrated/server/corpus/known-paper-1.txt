Decentralised ledgers promise a tamper evident record of every step in the peer review of a scientific manuscript, yet recording identities on such a ledger undermines the very anonymity that reviewers depend on. We therefore separate the permanent identity of a member from the pseudonym used inside a single publication session, and we ensure that no value stored on the ledger links the two. Each pseudonym signs its own actions, so the operator of the system can relay a transaction but cannot forge one. The evaluation shows that the overhead of the signature check is small compared with the cost of storing the manuscript hash, and that the number of transactions per session stays constant as the number of reviewers grows.
